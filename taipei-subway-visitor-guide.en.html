Taipei subway for first-time visitors: when do you need to leave?

The Taipei subway (the MRT) is easy to ride, but with transfers it is hard to guess how early to leave for a dinner reservation, a tour pickup or the last train of the night. These answers come from the App Store description of Metro Time Helper, an unofficial Taipei MRT app.

Plan a Taipei subway trip in three steps

  1. Choose your starting station and destination, or tap a station on the built-in zoomable MRT map.
  2. Enter the time you need to arrive.
  3. See the latest time to walk into the station and a step-by-step timeline for every leg and transfer. Would rather leave right now? Switch to Leave now and see when you will get there.

Two branch lines with their own timetable mode

  • Beitou to Xinbeitou: the short line to the Xinbeitou hot spring area.
  • Qizhang to Xiaobitan: trains run only about every 12 to 16 minutes.

Both use the official fixed timetable, with a countdown to the next train, full timetables for today, weekdays and weekends, departure reminders 1 to 30 minutes ahead, and the official timetable PDF.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Taipei subway the same as the Taipei MRT?

Yes. Locals call the Taipei subway the MRT. Metro Time Helper is built for the Taipei MRT and works backward from the time you need to arrive, transfers included, to tell you the latest time to walk into the station.

I have never ridden the Taipei subway. How do I know when to leave?

Choose your starting station, your destination and the time you need to arrive. You get the latest time to enter the station and a step-by-step timeline for every leg and transfer. You can also tap any station on the built-in zoomable MRT map to set it as your start or destination, and your route is drawn right on the map.

Which lines does it cover? Is the Airport MRT included?

Wenhu, Tamsui-Xinyi (including the Xinbeitou branch), Songshan-Xindian (including the Xiaobitan branch), Zhonghe-Xinlu, Bannan, phase one of the Circular line, and the New Taipei Metro Sanying line (Dingpu to Yingge). The Airport MRT and light rail are not included, so plan your airport trip separately.

I want to visit the Xinbeitou hot springs. Does it help with the branch line?

Yes. The short Beitou to Xinbeitou branch has its own mode on the official fixed timetable, with a countdown to the next train, full timetables for today, weekdays and weekends, departure reminders 1 to 30 minutes ahead, and the official timetable PDF.

Are the times live arrivals?

No. Times are estimates based on official published headways and travel times between stations, not live train arrivals. Pick Standard estimates (half a headway of waiting) or Cautious estimates (a full headway), and adjust your station entry buffer to leave yourself more margin.

What about the last train back to my hotel?

The trip timeline shows first and last train warnings, and once you set Remind me you get a reminder when it is time to go. A Live Activity counts down on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island (iPhone only).

Does it work in English or my own language?

Yes. The app comes in 50 languages you can switch inside the app. The official Taipei MRT network map is in the app as a zoomable vector map in Chinese or English (the map needs a connection).

Do I need an account? Is there a subscription?

No sign-up, no personal data collection, no ads and no third-party analytics. On the App Store it is a paid download: buy once and every feature is available from the very first launch, with no subscription and no in-app purchases. A paid version is also on Google Play; check its listing for Android details.

Is this an official Taipei subway app?

No. Metro Time Helper is an independent app and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Taipei MRT operator. It uses publicly available official data such as timetables, travel times and headways.
